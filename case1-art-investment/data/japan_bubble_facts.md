# Japan Asset Bubble → Art Market: Episode Facts for M3

**Why this is an episode study, not a regression:** no free downloadable annual art
index covers 1985-1999. The MM European index (our modern primary) starts at 2000.
M3 treats Japan as a documented crowded-trade crash (magnitudes + narrative), which is
the correct treatment for a one-marginal-buyer story. Directly answers brief Q9 (crowded
trade) and Q8 (correlations/risk hidden by short or smoothed indices).

## Key figures (sourced)

| Fact | Value | Use |
|------|-------|-----|
| Impressionist price run-up | **+940% over ~10 years** | Bubble magnitude |
| Japanese share of Impressionist market | **40-50% of works** | Crowded trade / one marginal buyer (Q9) |
| Trigger | 1985 yen revaluation (Plaza Accord) + US tax-law change removing donation incentive | Demand shock |
| Peak sales window | **1987-1990** (accelerated after the 1987 stock crash) | Timing |
| Peak marquee sales | May 1990: Saito buys Van Gogh *Dr. Gachet* **$82.5M** + Renoir *Bal du moulin* **$78.1M**; earlier Yasuda buys *Sunflowers* **~$39.9M** (1987) | Concreteness |
| Crash | From **1991** as Japan's stock/real-estate bubbles burst | Shock event |
| Drawdown | **Prices fell >50%** | Downside |
| Recovery | **~15 years** | Illiquidity / holding-period risk (Q6) |
| Root cause | One marginal buyer (Japanese capital) in one segment (Impressionism) | The thesis punchline |

## The Q8 point (index blindness)
Our modern anchor (MM European) and the investable Artprice100 both start at 2000 —
they **structurally cannot see** the largest art-market crash on record. That blindness
is itself the evidence for Q8: long/short indices hide black-swan correlations. State this
explicitly in the memo.
