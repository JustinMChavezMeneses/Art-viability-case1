ARTKAART provides no evidence of an inflation hedge. In the full 1980Q2–2026Q2 sample (n=185), the nominal-art inflation beta is −0.058 (HAC SE 0.325; t=−0.18 vs. zero) after controlling for the S&P 500; full CPI pass-through (β=1) is rejected (t=−3.26, p=0.001). The 2003Q2–2026Q2 specification that also controls for changes in 10-year breakevens is similarly near zero (β=0.040; HAC SE 0.368; β=1 p=0.009). The modern 2000+ beta is positive but small and imprecise (0.245; HAC SE 0.393), not statistically different from either zero or one at 5%; it does **not** establish a modern inflation hedge. Rather than showing a historical hedge that then collapses, these data fail to support full pass-through in either era. Real art returns fall as realized inflation rises (β=−1.014, HAC t=−2.97). M2 therefore **kills the inflation-hedge leg** of the proposed thesis, while not addressing art’s diversification or crisis properties.

## Caveats

- ARTDAI auction-index returns can be appraisal/transaction smoothed; HAC(4) addresses serially correlated inference, not index-construction bias.
- Inflation is annualized quarterly CPI log inflation, whereas nominal art and market returns are one-quarter log returns; the beta is a contemporaneous sensitivity, not a long-horizon purchasing-power test.
- The high-inflation subset has only 49 observations and a wide HAC confidence interval; it is directional evidence, not a precise tail-regime estimate.
- The 2000+ estimate is not precise enough to reject β=1 at the conventional 5% threshold (p=0.055), so the proper statement is “no evidence of pass-through,” not a definitive estimate of zero.
